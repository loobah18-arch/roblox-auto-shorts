# 03 — The 6-Step Workflow

## Step 1: Build a One-Page Campaign Sheet

Open the full brief **before** downloading anything. Create a note called `[CAMPAIGN NAME] — RULES` with:

**Economics**
- Rate/1K
- Budget remaining
- Minimum payout
- Max payout per post
- Flat-fee bonus
- Payment status

**Creative**
- Allowed assets
- Exact platforms
- Minimum/maximum length
- Required title
- Hashtags
- Caption copy
- Watermark
- Links
- Prohibited themes

## Step 2: Scan the Source for Story Moments

Watch source at **1.5× speed**. Mark timestamps when you see one of **five triggers**:

1. **The Surprise** — a statement/action that contradicts viewer expectations.
2. **The Stakes** — something that can be won, lost, exposed, proved, or failed.
3. **The Mechanism** — explains *how* something works, not just what happened.
4. **The Reveal** — viewer sees a product, result, reaction, transformation, or answer.
5. **The Useful Takeaway** — a lesson a viewer can remember without knowing the creator.

## Step 3: Use Claude to Rank Candidates

Paste the campaign-approved transcript into the **Clip Finder prompt** (`../prompts/clip-finder.md`). The goal is locating complete mini-stories you can verify in the raw footage — **not** generating viral clips.

## Step 4: Verify the Raw Clip

Claude identifies structure but **cannot reliably judge the visual reveal, facial reaction, or how a line was delivered**. For each top-5 candidate, play **20 seconds before and after**:

- Confirm the first sentence isn't taken out of context.
- Find the exact frame that proves or resolves the opening.
- Cut filler but don't cut information that makes the claim true.
- Reject clips that need a misleading headline to feel interesting.

## Step 5: Edit to Add Value

A campaign may grant an asset licence, but **platform originality policies still matter**. Add **one value layer** per clip: truthful question, explanatory voiceover, relevant graphic, sourced fact, or new narrative structure.

Timeline structure:
- **0–2 seconds:** Truthful hook — open a real question or tension.
- **2–22 seconds:** Forward motion — fresh detail, visual, or context every few seconds.
- **Final seconds:** Proof/payoff — close the question the hook created; don't cut the proof.

## Step 6: Post, Submit, and Log Immediately

Post only to campaign-accepted platforms. **Save the live URL and source timecode in your tracker before starting the next clip.**

**Post checklist:** Correct platform, aspect ratio, title, hashtags, caption, required watermark, disclosures, link, content length, no third-party watermark.

**Submission checklist:** Correct campaign, live URL, media file if requested, right account linked, required analytics attachment, approval status checked.

## Worked Example: Tech / Productivity Podcast Clip

**Scenario:** A founder explains why their first launch underperformed.

| Timestamp | Beat |
|-----------|------|
| 12:14 | Founder states only **71 people** signed up for their launch |
| 12:21 | Reading support emails — recurring line: users **didn't know what to do first** |
| 12:32 | They removed **six dashboard tabs**, added one **"Start here"** screen |
| 12:43 | Next cohort finished onboarding more often; conclusion: **"Confusion is a product bug"** |

**Edit plan:**
- Opening text: a contrarian lesson about *confusing vs. broken* products.
- Start clip from the failure admission ("We thought the launch failed…").
- Value layer: voiceover — *their first instinct was more traffic, but the emails showed a different problem.*
- Visual proof: graphic showing **6 choices → 1 clear first action**.
- Payoff: the "Confusion is a product bug" line.

**Critical constraint:** Do **not** claim unproven results — do not say they "doubled conversions" or "made them millions." The source proves neither.
